ChartR builds realistic healthcare environments and clinically meaningful tasks for evaluating models.

An environment is a working system — a clinical record, a payer integration, a research database, a phone line — seeded with data and wired so that a model's actions have real consequences inside it. A model is measured on what it accomplishes, not on what it says it would do.

## How our environments are built

<Steps>
  <Step title="A real system, not a transcript">
    Each environment runs actual software — a FHIR server, an EHR, a payer API, an imaging archive. A model's writes persist and its mistakes have consequences.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A surface chosen to isolate a capability">
    The same clinical work is offered through curated tools, raw protocols, or a screen, depending on what the environment is meant to measure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Grading on end state, not narration">
    Wherever possible a model is graded on what exists in the system when it finishes — the orders placed, the claim adjudicated, the record filed — rather than on its description of what it did.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Clinically meaningful failure modes">
    Tasks are built around the errors that matter in practice: anchoring on the chart instead of the evidence, disclosing before verifying, recommending without executing.
  </Step>
</Steps>
