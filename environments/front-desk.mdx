A medical receptionist answers a call. Somebody needs a prescription resent to the right pharmacy, or their results, or a records copy, and the model has to hold the conversation and do the clinical administrative work at the same time.

This is the only environment where a model is doing two things at once under real constraints: talking to a person who is waiting, and working a chart that has to end up correct.

## What the model works with

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Phone control" icon="phone-volume">
    Answer, speak, place on hold, resume, transfer, and end the call.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Full chart access" icon="folder-open">
    The clinical record, for the work the call is actually about.
  </Card>

## The caller is deterministic

The person on the other end is not improvising. The same questions produce the same disclosed facts, every run.

More importantly, **facts are released only when actually asked for**. A caller does not volunteer their date of birth because the model needs it; the model has to ask. That makes identity verification something the environment can measure directly, rather than something that happens to occur.

## Conduct gates

<Warning>
  Several conduct failures zero the score outright, regardless of whether the task was completed.
</Warning>

A model that resends the prescription correctly but disclosed protected information before verifying who it was talking to has not done the job. Neither has one that acted on a caller's assertion without a corresponding signal in the chart, or modified something it had no business touching.

This is deliberate and it is the most opinionated scoring in the catalogue. In a front-office setting, some failures are not partial credit — they are the whole outcome.

## Task families

Prescription routing, refills, and non-receipt · results release · records requests and copies · telehealth registration · imaging copies · specimen retest · insurance and coverage · referral follow-up · portal access · paediatric calls involving a guardian.
